Project Design Document: Campaign & System Overview

1. Narrative & Worldbuilding Overview

1.1 Protagonist & Motivation

  - Identity: You are an on-site Depthworks security guard armed with a
    standard-issue shotgun.
  - Inciting Incident: During a catastrophic facility collapse, an elevator
    failure dropped you deep into high-clearance sub-levels well beyond your
    authorized security tier.
  - The Mission: Returning to the facility immediately in the wake of the
    disaster to locate surviving coworkers—specifically your close friend.
  - The Breadcrumb: At the moment of the incident, your friend was stationed at
    her terminal sending messages. Finding her begins by retracing her steps to
    her last known position: her workstation terminal.
  - Progression Route: Accessing deeper sectors requires recovering localized
    media (e.g., floppy disks) and overriding electronic access computers (such
    as the reactor floor terminal), bypassing traditional card-reader
    infrastructure you no longer have access to.

1.2 The Conflict: Depthworks Cleanup Force

  - The Lockdown: The entire subterranean complex has been placed under complete
    isolation to eliminate witnesses and suppress information leaks.
  - The Adversaries: Depthworks has deployed an elite internal security/cleanup
    force. These are professionals you know and recognize. They are actively
    executing surviving workers and researchers.
  - The Dynamic: Because you wear the uniform, you are technically one of them.
    However, they quickly recognize you were not assigned to the cleanup roster.
    If you catch isolated guards unaware, you can use your identity, stealth,
    and lethal force to survive.

2. Core Gameplay Systems

2.1 Combat & Arsenal

  - Primary Firearm: Shotgun (starting weapon).
  - Future Additions: Additional kinetic, tactical, and facility-grade weapons
    to be introduced throughout later chapters.

2.2 Stealth & Physical Interaction

  - Stance: Crouching lowers player profile and reduces footstep noise.
  - Distraction Mechanic: Throwing physics-enabled objects while crouched lures
    guards away from patrol paths and forces an alert/distracted state.
  - Takedown System (From Distracted/Unaware State):
      - Non-Lethal: Knock unconscious.
      - Tactical: Grapple enemy to use as a human shield.
      - Lethal: Snap neck.

2.3 Tools & Traversal

  - Flashlight Mechanic: Keybound to [F]. System logic, battery/toggle states,
    and lighting passes are functional; pending final 3D asset integration.
  - Access Progression: Physical clearance relies on industrial workarounds
    (floppy media, administrative override consoles, mechanical overrides)
    rather than standard high-tier keycards.

3. Pacing Philosophy

  - Avoid Over-Pacing: Do not front-load 40+ consecutive massive narrative
    beats. The facility must feel vast, functional, and lived-in.
      - Early Maps (e.g., Maps 5–7): Environmental storytelling showing routine
        shifts, sudden evacuation, failing automation, and finding routes.
      - Delayed Payoffs: Foreshadowing established early (such as the "last
        train arrived empty" clue) should pay off dozens of maps later (e.g.,
        Map 16).
      - Pacing Valleys: Sequences like Maps 30–35 give the player room to
        breathe through industrial exploration before ramping back into hostile
        territory.
  - The Architecture of Secrecy (Maps 37–44): The transition from industrial
    infrastructure to clinical, restricted architecture visually informs the
    player that ordinary employees were never permitted near the core operation.
  - The Reveal of the Anomaly: Show what humans were doing (sampling, measuring,
    extracting power, constructing containment) long before exposing the
    cosmology of what lies beneath.

5. Production Notes & Next Steps

  - Medical Facilities: Formally positioned in Map 48 (Medical Screening) within
    the Research sector, with preliminary aid outposts planned for Map 33
    (Maintenance District).
  - Placeholder Policy: Names in Chapter 4 (such as Machine Shrine and Crystal /
    Stone Galleries) function as tonal placeholders to guide visual development,
    leaving narrative definitions open to iteration during blockout.
  - Asset Priorities:
    1.  Flashlight hand/weapon viewmodel (to replace temporary blank anchor).
    2.  Pickup interaction prompts for floppy disks and system terminals.
    3.  Enemy AI state machine hooks for the crouch-throw distraction response.
